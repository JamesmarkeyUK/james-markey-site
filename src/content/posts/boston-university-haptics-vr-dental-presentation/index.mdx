This week I had the enormous privilege of presenting at **Boston University** in the United States on my specialist subject — the use of **haptics** (the sense of touch) and **VR** (immersive virtual reality) in dental education.

It was a particularly special occasion for me, as this was my **first presentation since being appointed a Member of the Order of the British Empire (MBE)** in His Majesty The King's Birthday Honours 2026. To carry that honour into a room full of educators, clinicians and students — and to talk about the work I care so deeply about — was a moment I will not forget.

The heart of the talk was simple: by bringing the sense of touch into the virtual realm, we can let aspiring dentists "feel" the intricate nuances of a procedure — drilling, probing, and correcting their technique — in an environment that mirrors reality, all before they ever touch a real patient. It is risk-free, repeatable, and measurable, and it is changing what is possible in dental training.

This is the work we do every day at [UNI SIM](https://www.unisim.co.uk) and at [Virteasy Dental](https://www.virteasy.com), and it is always humbling to share it with an audience as engaged as the one at Boston University. To do so on the international stage — championing British innovation abroad — is exactly the kind of responsibility I hope to keep living up to.

My heartfelt thanks to Boston University for the warm welcome and the invitation, and to everyone who came along, asked questions and joined the conversation. The future of dental education is hands-on, immersive, and global — and I cannot wait to see where it goes next.
